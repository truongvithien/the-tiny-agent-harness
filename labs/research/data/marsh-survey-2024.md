# Blackwater Marsh Heron Survey 2024

Conflicts-With: regional-bird-atlas

The 2024 dawn survey counted 128 active grey heron nesting pairs across the
four heronries of the Blackwater Marsh reserve. Counting ran from 4 April to
19 April 2024, the window the reserve uses every year because occupied nests
stay visible before the alder canopy closes over the carr.

Observers walked the fixed transect at first light on eight mornings and
recorded a nest as active only when an adult was seen standing on it or
delivering material to it. Two observers counted each heronry independently,
and the reserve accepted a heronry total only when the two counts agreed to
within three nests.

The 2024 figure is higher than every total the reserve has published since it
began the transect in 2011. The reserve attributes part of the rise to the
2022 removal of two drainage sluices, which raised the water table in the
northern carr and reopened feeding channels that had dried out.

Three heronries were counted on all eight mornings. The fourth, in the flooded
willow scrub east of the boardwalk, could be seen from the boardwalk on only
five mornings, so its total carries a wider margin than the other three.
